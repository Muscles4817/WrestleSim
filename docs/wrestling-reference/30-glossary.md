# 30 — Glossary

*Industry vocabulary, including the words that mean two or three different things.*

Terms marked **[carny]** come from the carnival origins of the business and are part of the
trade's private language.

---

## A

**Angle** — A storyline segment; a non-match piece of storytelling. Also used for the
storyline itself.

**Apuesta** — (Lucha) A bet match: mask vs mask, hair vs hair, or a combination. Permanent
and legally recognised in Mexico.

**Ace** — (Japan) The promotion's central star; the standard against which everyone is
measured. A formal-ish position, not just a description.

**Agent** — See *Producer*. The older term.

**Alignment** — Whether a character is a face, heel, or tweener.

**Angle shot** — A televised segment designed to advance a story rather than to be a match.

**Apron** — The canvas edge outside the ropes.

**Armdrag / arm wringer / etc.** — Basic wrestling holds; the vocabulary of the "chain
wrestling" sequence.

**Attitude Era** — WWF, roughly 1997–2001. Edgier content, peak business.

---

## B

**Babyface / face** — The character the audience is meant to cheer.

**Blade / blading / getting colour** — Deliberately cutting oneself to bleed. Restricted or
banned in several promotions.

**Blow-off** — The final, decisive match of a feud.

**Book** — (verb) To decide what happens. (noun) The creative plan. "Holding the book" =
being the booker.

**Booker** — The person who decides the creative direction and match results.

**Botch** — A mistake in execution.

**Broadway** — A time-limit draw. From "Broadway show", i.e. it went the distance.

**Bump** — Taking a fall. "Bump card" = the physical toll.

**Bury / burial** — Booking someone into irrelevance, perceived as deliberate.

**Business, the** — Professional wrestling as a trade. "Protecting the business" =
maintaining kayfabe.

---

## C

**Call / calling the match** — Communicating during the match about what happens next.
"Calling it in the ring" = improvising the match live.

**Card** — The lineup of matches for a show.

**Card position** — Where on the card someone is placed; the status hierarchy.

**Carny** — The carnival culture and language of the business. Also, someone who behaves
in the manipulative style associated with it.

**Cheap heat** — Booing generated by insulting the town or the audience rather than by
character work. Effective, shallow, instantly renewable.

**Clean finish** — A decisive win with no interference or cheating.

**Colour** — Blood. "Getting colour" = bleeding.

**Comeback** — The babyface's rally after the heat section.

**Cool heel** — A heel the audience admires rather than hates. Structurally problematic.

**Creative** — The department, or the plan. "Creative has nothing for you" = the standard
phrase preceding a release.

**Crossover** — (Brand splits) A performer appearing on the other brand's show.

**Cut-off** — The moment the heel stops the babyface's early control.

---

## D

**Dark match** — An untelevised match at a TV taping, usually before the broadcast (to warm
the crowd) or after (to send them home happy).

**Dirt sheet** — A wrestling news publication reporting backstage information. Originally
pejorative, now largely neutral.

**Downside guarantee** — The minimum annual payment in a contract, regardless of dates
worked.

**Draw** — (verb) To attract paying customers. (noun) Someone who does. **The only metric
that ultimately matters commercially.**

**Duelling chants** — Two competing chants from the crowd. A sign of maximum engagement.

**Dusty finish** — A finish where the babyface appears to win but the decision is reversed
on a technicality. Named for Dusty Rhodes, who used it heavily. Protects both, frustrates
the audience.

---

## E

**Enhancement talent** — A performer whose role is to lose and make others look good.
Formerly "jobber".

**Excursion** — (Japan) Sending a young wrestler abroad for 1–3 years to develop, returning
repackaged and elevated.

---

## F

**Face** — See *Babyface*.

**Face in peril** — The dominant match structure: the babyface takes an extended beating
before a comeback.

**Feud** — A sustained rivalry between characters.

**Finish** — How the match ends.

**Finisher** — A performer's signature match-ending move.

**Freshness** — How novel something still feels. Decays with use.

---

## G

**Gate** — Ticket revenue (as distinct from *house*, which is the number of people).

**Gimmick** — (1) A character. (2) A prop or weapon. (3) A stipulation. **[carny]** —
Context disambiguates.

**Gimmick table** — Where an independent wrestler sells their own merchandise.

**Go-away heat** — Boos that mean "get off my television" rather than "I want to see you
beaten". A failure state, not heat.

**Go home** — (1) To finish the match. (2) The "go-home show" — the last TV before a
pay-per-view.

**Gorilla position** — The area behind the entrance curtain where the head of production
sits with monitors. Named after Gorilla Monsoon. **The command centre of a live show.**

**Green** — Inexperienced.

---

## H

**Hard camera** — The main fixed camera. The side of the arena it faces is dressed to look
full.

**Hard out** — The time the broadcast must end.

**Heat** — (1) Audience hatred for a heel. (2) The accumulated tension in a feud.
(3) Backstage trouble between people. **Three distinct meanings.**

**Heat section / heat segment** — The portion of a match where the heel is in control.

**Heel** — The character the audience is meant to boo.

**High spot** — A big, visually impressive move.

**Hope spot** — A brief babyface rally during the heat section that fails.

**Hot tag** — The moment a beaten wrestler reaches their fresh tag partner. One of the most
reliable pop mechanisms in wrestling.

**House** — (1) The attendance. (2) A house show.

**House show** — An untelevised live event.

---

## J

**Job / jobbing** — Losing, by design. "Doing the job."

**Jobber** — Older term for enhancement talent.

**Juice** — Blood, or (older usage) steroids. Context disambiguates.

---

## K

**Kayfabe** — The presentation of wrestling as genuine; the fiction and the code of
protecting it. **[carny]** Historically absolute; now largely a stylistic choice.

**Kick-out** — Escaping a pinfall before three.

---

## L

**Lucha libre** — Mexican professional wrestling, with its own conventions.

**Legend** — A retired or semi-retired star used as an attraction.

---

## M

**Manager** — A non-wrestling character who accompanies and speaks for a performer.

**Mark** — (1) A fan who believes it is real. (2) More broadly, anyone emotionally invested.
(3) A promoter who overspends out of fandom. **[carny]** Originally the carnival term for a
target.

**Mid-card** — The middle of the roster hierarchy.

**Money in the Bank** — A briefcase containing a guaranteed, cashable title shot.

**Monday Night Wars** — The 1995–2001 ratings competition between Raw and Nitro.

---

## N

**Near fall** — A pinfall attempt broken at the last moment.

**No-sell** — Failing to react to a move as if it hurt. Usually a criticism; in strong style
it is a deliberate convention.

**nWo** — New World Order. The 1996–99 WCW faction; the highest-drawing angle in American
wrestling history.

---

## O

**Over** — The state of the audience caring about you. The fundamental measure.

**Overness** — How over someone is.

---

## P

**Papering** — Giving away tickets to fill a building.

**PLE** — Premium Live Event. WWE's post-PPV terminology for a major show.

**Pop** — A sudden loud cheer.

**Producer** — Former wrestler who lays out matches with the performers and relays creative's
intent. Also called an agent.

**Program** — A storyline between two performers, roughly synonymous with feud but often
implying a planned run of matches.

**Puroresu** — Japanese professional wrestling.

**Push** — A sustained allocation of resources to elevate a performer.

---

## R

**Rate** — How a match is judged for quality, as distinct from how it draws.

**Receipt** — A retaliatory stiff shot for one received.

**Repackage** — Giving a performer a new character.

**Rest hold** — A hold used to slow the pace and let performers recover.

**Rib** — A practical joke. **[carny]** A long-standing locker-room institution.

**Ring psychology** — The logic that makes a match cohere. See
[18-match-craft.md](18-match-craft.md).

**Rocket push** — A rapid elevation.

**Run-in** — Interference during a match.

**Rudo** — (Lucha) The rough/rulebreaking side. Approximately a heel.

**Rundown / format sheet** — The segment-by-segment plan for a show.

---

## S

**Screwjob** — A finish involving deception; specifically, the Montreal Screwjob (1997).

**Sell / selling** — Reacting to offence so that it looks damaging. **The most important
in-ring skill.**

**Shine** — The babyface's early control section of a match.

**Shoot** — Something genuine rather than worked. **[carny]** "Shoot interview" = an
out-of-character interview. Opposite of *work*.

**Smark** — "Smart mark" — a fan who knows the business is worked and follows it as such.

**Spot** — A planned sequence or move.

**Spot monkey** — Pejorative for a wrestler who does spots without psychology.

**Squash** — A one-sided, very short match.

**Stiff** — Hitting harder than necessary.

**Stipulation** — A special rule or stake attached to a match.

**Strong style** — The Japanese style emphasising stiff strikes and fighting spirit.

**Super-indie** — A large independent promotion with international reputation.

**Swerve** — An unexpected reversal.

---

## T

**Taking a liberty** — Hitting someone harder than agreed, or otherwise abusing the
cooperative trust.

**Talent relations** — The department managing contracts and the roster.

**Técnico** — (Lucha) The clean/rulefollowing side. Approximately a babyface.

**Territory** — A geographically exclusive promotion under the old NWA system.

**Time limit draw** — Neither wins within the allotted time.

**Tolling** — A contract clause freezing and extending the term during injury or absence.

**Transitional champion** — Someone given a title briefly to move it between two others.

**Tryout** — A multi-day evaluation camp.

**Turn** — A change of alignment.

**Tweener** — Neither clearly face nor heel.

---

## U

**Unit** — (Japan) A faction. NJPW's primary roster organising structure.

**Unprotected chair shot** — A chair shot to the head with no blocking. Banned by WWE from
2007.

**Over-sell** — Reacting excessively. Occasionally a criticism, occasionally the point.

---

## V

**Vignette** — A pre-taped character package, usually to introduce or reintroduce someone.

---

## W

**Work** — (noun) Something planned/predetermined. (verb) To wrestle, or to deceive.
**[carny]** "Working a match", "working the crowd", "working someone" — all related senses.

**Worked shoot** — A planned segment presented as if it were genuine. The most potent and
most dangerous tool available.

**Workrate** — Athletic quality and output of in-ring work.

**Worker** — A wrestler, specifically as an in-ring performer. "A good worker" is a
technical compliment, not a commercial one.

---

## X

**X-Pac heat** — See *go-away heat*. Named for a period in which the performer X-Pac drew
boos of the "please leave" variety.

---

## Y

**Young Lion** — (Japan) A dojo trainee working the undercard in plain black trunks,
expected to lose for years.

---
