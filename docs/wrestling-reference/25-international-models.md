# 25 — International Models

*Japan, Mexico, the UK and Europe, and elsewhere — genuinely different operating systems,
not regional variations on the American model.*

---

## 1. Why this matters

The American model — weekly episodic television, character-driven storytelling, a
face/heel morality axis, tentpole PPVs — is one option among several. Other traditions
solve the same problems differently, and several of their solutions are better.

For anyone modelling wrestling, these are not flavour. They are **alternative rule sets**.

---

## 2. Japan (Puroresu)

### 2.1 The structural differences

| Dimension | Japan | United States |
|---|---|---|
| **Schedule unit** | The **tour** (8–20 consecutive dates ending in a big show) | The **week** (episodic TV) |
| **Story engine** | **Tournaments and rankings** | **Feuds and angles** |
| **Presentation** | Sport | Entertainment |
| **Character** | The wrestler is mostly themselves; character is secondary | Central |
| **Won-loss records** | Tracked, meaningful, referenced | Largely ignored |
| **Face/heel** | Softer; often a matter of unit affiliation and style | Central axis |
| **Promos** | Brief, post-match, in the ring or backstage | Extensive, produced |
| **Match length** | Long; 20–35 minute main events are routine | Shorter on TV |
| **Crowd behaviour** | Attentive, quiet during holds, explosive at near-falls | Continuous noise |
| **Television** | Highlights packages, streaming; not the primary narrative vehicle | The narrative vehicle |

### 2.2 The tournament system
The central creative technology of Japanese wrestling.

**The G1 Climax (NJPW):** roughly 20 wrestlers in two blocks, round-robin, one month,
~19–20 shows. Every match matters because points matter. The winner gets the main event of
Wrestle Kingdom on January 4.

**Why it is so effective:**
- Generates months of meaningful matches with **no angles required**
- Creates natural upsets that make stars automatically
- Produces a clear, legible narrative anyone can follow
- Builds to a predetermined destination five months in advance
- Puts the entire roster on the same stage

**Other league/tournament formats:** Champion Carnival (AJPW), Best of the Super Juniors,
World Tag League, New Japan Cup, King of Pro-Wrestling, N-1 Victory (NOAH).

### 2.3 The dojo and Young Lion system
See [10-talent-acquisition.md](10-talent-acquisition.md) §4.3. The key elements:
- Live-in training, extreme conditioning, strict seniority
- Young Lions wear plain black trunks, work the openers, and lose for years
- **The excursion:** sent abroad for 1–3 years, returns with a new name, look, and character,
  immediately elevated

**The excursion is the best star-making device in wrestling** and has no American equivalent.

### 2.4 The unit system
Rather than brands, NJPW organises its roster into factions/units: CHAOS, Bullet Club,
Los Ingobernables de Japón, United Empire, House of Torture, and so on. Units:
- Give every roster member an identity
- Create automatic alliances and rivalries
- Make multi-man tag matches (the staple of tour undercards) meaningful
- Provide a mechanism for turns (leaving/joining a unit)

### 2.5 The undercard tag structure
On a tour, most performers work multi-man tags every night that **build to the singles
matches at the tour's finale**. This is elegant: it gets everyone on every show, keeps
bodies fresh, previews the big matches, and gives the crowd a reason to watch the undercard.

### 2.6 Strong style and the "fighting spirit" convention
A convention where a wrestler no-sells a big move to demonstrate willpower. This directly
contradicts American selling doctrine and is genuinely a different aesthetic, not a mistake.
It works within its own grammar and reads as poor psychology when imported without context.

### 2.7 Joshi (women's wrestling)
Japan has an independent, deep, historically significant women's wrestling tradition
(All Japan Women's in the 1980s–90s; Stardom, TJPW, and others today) that at points
operated at a level of athleticism and drawing power ahead of the men's product. AJW's
1990s peak — drawing large crowds with a workrate standard the rest of the world took a
decade to reach — is a genuinely important and often-omitted part of wrestling history.

## 3. Mexico (Lucha Libre)

### 3.1 The structural differences
| Dimension | Lucha |
|---|---|
| **Default match format** | **Trios** (three-a-side), not singles |
| **Fall structure** | Best of three falls, traditionally |
| **Alignment** | **Técnico / Rudo** — closer to "clean/rough" than "good/evil" |
| **The mask** | Identity, not costume. Legally registered. Losing it is permanent. |
| **The stipulation** | *Apuesta* — mask vs mask, hair vs hair. The highest stake in wrestling. |
| **Schedule** | Fixed weekly dates at fixed venues, for decades |
| **Regulation** | The Mexico City Boxing and Wrestling Commission has statutory authority |
| **Style** | Fast, aerial, rope-running sequences, elaborate exchanges |
| **Family lineage** | Extremely strong; names pass down generations |

### 3.2 The mask
The single most distinctive feature of lucha libre. A masked wrestler's identity is
protected — legally registered with the commission, not revealed publicly, and treated with
genuine seriousness. Consequences:
- **A mask loss is irreversible.** You cannot re-mask (except under specific traditional
  circumstances). This makes it the only genuinely permanent stipulation in wrestling.
- **Mask vs mask matches are the biggest draws in Mexican wrestling**, sometimes built over
  years.
- The mask is also a commercial asset — merchandise, iconography, cultural presence far
  beyond wrestling.
- **A masked wrestler can be replaced**, and characters have outlived their original
  performers (the Mil Máscaras/Blue Demon/Santo lineages).

### 3.3 The trios format
Three-a-side is the *default*, not a novelty. This changes everything:
- More people on every card
- Rapid tag rules (touching the floor eliminates a partner) that allow constant motion
- Story told through the group dynamic
- Singles matches become special by contrast

### 3.4 CMLL and AAA
- **CMLL** (1933–) is the oldest continuously operating promotion in the world. Fixed weekly
  schedule at Arena México, deeply traditional, family-owned across four generations,
  conservative in presentation.
- **AAA** (1992–) was founded as a more television-friendly, spectacle-forward alternative,
  and was acquired by WWE in 2025.

### 3.5 Why lucha matters structurally
It demonstrates that a completely different grammar — different default match format,
different alignment axis, different stipulation economy, different star-identity model —
can sustain a mass audience for ninety years. Any argument that the American conventions
are "how wrestling works" is refuted by Arena México on a Friday night.

## 4. The United Kingdom and Europe

### 4.1 The World of Sport era (1955–1988)
British wrestling on ITV, Saturday afternoons, drawing audiences of 10–12 million at its
peak. Structural features:
- **Round-based**: 5-minute rounds with breaks, like boxing
- **Points scoring**: falls, submissions, and knockouts scored; matches won on points
- **Weight classes** taken seriously
- **A technical, mat-based style** ("British catch") with a distinctive vocabulary of holds
- **A referee with genuine authority**, issuing public warnings
- Stars: Big Daddy, Giant Haystacks, Kendo Nagasaki, Mick McManus

**Its death:** ITV dropped it in 1988. The scene collapsed and took roughly 25 years to
rebuild. A clean demonstration that a national wrestling culture can be destroyed by a
single broadcaster decision.

### 4.2 The modern British scene
Rebuilt from the 2000s: Progress, RevPro, ICW, Fight Club Pro, and a generation of
performers who became internationally significant. WWE's NXT UK (2018–2022) consolidated
much of it under contract and then vacated, which is a case study in how a major's entry
and exit distorts a local ecosystem.

### 4.3 Continental Europe
- **Germany:** wXw (Westside Xtreme Wrestling) as the anchor; a strong tournament tradition
  (16 Carat Gold); a long history of tournament-format wrestling going back to the
  turn-of-the-century *Ringkampf* circuit
- **Historic:** the European catch-as-catch-can tradition, the German tournament system,
  and the French *catch* television era, all of which fed into the technical style

## 5. Elsewhere

| Region | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Canada** | Historically Stampede (Calgary, the Hart family) and Maple Leaf (Toronto); now largely absorbed into the North American circuit |
| **Australia** | A live-event market rather than a production centre; strong touring destination |
| **Puerto Rico** | WWC and the Colón family; a genuinely distinct territory tradition with an intense live culture |
| **India** | Repeatedly targeted as a growth market; several attempts at a domestic major; large WWE viewership without a sustained local industry |
| **Saudi Arabia** | Not a promotion but a **buyer** — site fees for major events; see [08](08-television-and-media.md) §6 |
| **China** | Repeatedly attempted, no sustained success |

## 6. What the American model could learn (and periodically does)

| Import | From | Adopted as |
|---|---|---|
| Tournament story engines | Japan | King of the Ring, Money in the Bank, the various cups |
| The excursion | Japan | Rarely; the closest is a developmental repackage |
| Trios/multi-man as a default | Mexico | War Games, multi-man tags |
| Permanent-stake stipulations | Mexico | Career vs career, "loser leaves town" |
| Round/points structures | UK | Iron Man matches |
| Unit/faction organisation | Japan | Factions generally |
| Long-match sports presentation | Japan | NXT's early identity; AEW's stated ethos |

## 7. Common misreadings to avoid

- **"Japanese crowds are dead."** They are attentive. Silence during a hold is respect and
  concentration; the explosion at a near-fall is proportionally enormous. Modelling crowd
  reaction as a single loudness value will get Japan wrong.
- **"Lucha has no psychology."** It has a different psychology — group-based, momentum-based,
  built around trios dynamics and fall structure.
- **"British wrestling was slow."** It was rule-bound and technical, which is different.
- **"Puroresu is just workrate."** The best Japanese wrestling is intensely
  story-driven; the story is career-scale (the ace, the challenger, the record) rather than
  angle-scale.

## Sim implications

- **Regional rule sets as selectable game modes** would be genuinely distinctive: a Japanese
  mode with tours, tournaments, units, and a dojo; a lucha mode with trios, masks, and
  apuestas; a British mode with rounds and points.
- **The tournament as a story engine** is directly implementable and would give a booker a
  tool that generates stakes without requiring angles — mechanically distinct from the
  feud-based loop the sim already has.
- **The mask as a permanent, stakeable asset** is a beautiful mechanic: an irreversible
  stipulation with enormous payoff and no take-backs.
- **Trios as a first-class match format**, not a variant, changes card composition entirely.
- **Crowd reaction as a culture-dependent function**: the same match should produce different
  reaction profiles in Tokyo, Mexico City, and Philadelphia. This ties directly into the
  crowd-composition modifiers in [16-crowd-psychology.md](16-crowd-psychology.md).
- **The excursion** as a developmental mechanic (send a young talent away, they return
  repackaged with better stats and reset freshness) is the single most transplantable idea
  in this document.
